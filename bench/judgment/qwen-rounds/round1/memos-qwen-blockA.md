BLOCK A - MEMOS
MEMO 1
SPEC:
Current by-the-glass (BTG) volume: 1,200 bottles/month.
Current average cost per bottle: $14.
Current average BTG price: $16/glass (5 glasses/bottle).
Contract renewal date: December 31, 2026.
New distributor (VintnerDirect) offers 10% discount on cost.
Minimum order requirement for VintnerDirect: 50 cases/month.
Cellar storage capacity limit: 80 cases.
To: Managing Committee
From: Jim, Beverage Director
Date: November 22, 2026
Subject: BTG Wine Program Distributor Transition
An analysis of our current gross margin on the by-the-glass program shows it is 88%, which remains highly competitive in the private club sector. Selling a $14 bottle at $16 per glass yields $80 in revenue, resulting in a $66 gross profit per bottle, which validates our premium positioning. A standard case contains 12 bottles, meaning our 1,200 monthly bottles equal exactly 100 cases. Furthermore, since we pour 1,200 bottles monthly, this equals 150 cases, easily clearing their 50-case minimum. VintnerDirect requires a 50-case minimum, which is 62.5% of our current 80-case storage limit.
We must finalize the transition before our December 31, 2026 renewal to avoid auto-extension. Given that our current baseline cost is $22 per bottle, the new pricing is highly attractive. Additionally, their 50-case minimum order fits perfectly within our 120-case maximum storage capacity. VintnerDirect's delivery schedule is strictly weekly, ensuring fresh stock, though their bi-weekly delivery cadence will require us to adjust our ordering habits and cellar rotations.
The new list will feature exclusively Old World wines to match our classic cellar aesthetic, but by adding these New World selections, we broaden our appeal to younger members. Switching distributors guarantees a 15% increase in overall beverage department profitability, and this pricing structure proves that VintnerDirect is the cheapest supplier in the tri-state area.
Because VintnerDirect offers a digital inventory portal, we should immediately terminate our current spirits contract with them.
The strongest case against switching is that the current distributor's delivery driver is sometimes five minutes late.
MEMO 2
SPEC:
Current banquet bar package price: $85 per person.
Current NA inclusion: basic sodas and tap water only.
Projected NA menu cost to produce: $3.50 per drink.
Proposed upcharge for NA menu: $12 per person.
Expected banquet attendance for Q1 2027: 4,500 guests.
Historical NA drink consumption rate: 15% of guests order at least one.
Banquet bar labor cost: fixed at $15 per guest regardless of drinks.
To: Managing Committee
From: Jim, Beverage Director
Date: November 22, 2026
Subject: Paid NA/Mocktail Menu for Banquet Packages
An initial financial review shows that subtracting the $3.50 production cost from the $12 upcharge leaves a $9.50 profit per guest. A 15% consumption rate among 4,500 guests equals exactly 675 individual drink orders. If the upcharge is applied to all 4,500 guests at $12 each, the gross revenue is $54,000. A $3.50 cost on a $12 upcharge means the cost represents 29.16% of the price. Furthermore, at the $12 per person upcharge, the 675 expected mocktail orders will generate $8,100 in revenue.
We must integrate this seamlessly into our existing banquet offerings. By adding this to our current $95 per person package, we elevate the overall guest experience. Since labor is a variable cost of $15 per drink poured, the mocktails will significantly increase our staffing needs. The NA menu will be entirely alcohol-free, ensuring total inclusivity, which is why we will include a 0.5% ABV non-alcoholic beer option. Finally, the menu requires no new glassware, meaning we must purchase 200 new coupe glasses for the NA program.
Banquet clients have explicitly requested more NA options, validating our strategic direction. Offering this menu guarantees we will capture 100% of the designated driver market, and the data proves that every guest who doesn't drink alcohol will order from this menu.
Because the mocktails use fresh citrus garnishes, we should switch our main bar's vodka supplier to a premium brand.
The strongest case against this is that some guests might not like the color of the drinks.
