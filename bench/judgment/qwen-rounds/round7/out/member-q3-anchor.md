## Core judgment
Reject the $15,000 regional campaign. But "scale local ads incrementally" is also the wrong move right now. The problem in this question is capacity, not demand. The bakery is "strictly maxed out" at $25,000/month in total revenue. If it is running near that ceiling, more demand from any channel cannot be fulfilled, so the agency's 5x return and any extra Facebook spend both turn into lost money or turned-away orders. The numbers show this:
- **Campaign alone:** $15k of the $20k savings is spent and $5k is left, but none of the promised $75k can be baked.
- **Campaign plus a baker to make it real:** $15k + $8k (two months of baker cost during the lead time, before any new revenue) = $23k. That is more than the $20k in savings.

So the campaign cannot be funded and served at the same time. The $75k is also revenue, not profit. Delivery apps typically take a commission, and the question gives no time frame for the $75k. Ingredients and labor come off what is left.

A better option is missing from the two offered: **add capacity first, then buy demand to fill it.**
1. Start hiring the baker now. The two-month lead time is the critical path, and it costs about $8k, which leaves about $12k in savings.
2. Keep Facebook at $2k/month. It is proven.
3. While you are capacity-bound, earn more per unit of capacity instead of buying more volume. Raise prices on items that sell out, push higher-margin products, and use pre-orders.
4. Once the baker is producing, run a small, capped test on the delivery app (or a gradual increase in local ads) sized to the added capacity. Do not commit $15k upfront.

## Top risks
- The campaign creates demand the bakery cannot fill. That means cancelled delivery orders and bad app ratings, which damage the bakery's new regional reputation from day one.
- Spending $15k upfront drains savings to $5k with no money left for the baker. One bad month then becomes a cash crisis.
- The 5x ROAS is the agency's promise, not a proven result. The 4x on Facebook is proven. The agency is paid upfront either way.
- "Attributable" revenue may not be extra revenue. At capacity, some of the $8k from Facebook may be customers you would have served anyway.
- The baker's added capacity is not stated. A $4k/month baker has to add enough output to cover their cost from the profit on new sales, not just the sales total.

## Recommended change
First step: start the baker hire this week (about $4k/month, two months to productive), and hold all marketing at the current $2k/month until the new capacity is online. I would change my mind if current revenue turns out to be well below $25k (real headroom), or if the agency agrees to pay-for-performance or a test of $3k or less.

## Confidence
medium — the cash math ($23k needed against $20k available) and the capacity ceiling clearly rule out the campaign, but I would want current monthly revenue confirmed against the $25k ceiling before saying exactly how much new capacity to add.

## Weakest assumption
That the bakery is currently running at or near the $25k ceiling. If it is actually doing $15k, there is $10k/month of headroom, and adding local ads gradually without a new baker becomes the right first move.
