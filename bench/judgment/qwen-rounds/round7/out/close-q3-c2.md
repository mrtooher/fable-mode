## Your call and this memo

Your call: "Approve the $15,000 regional campaign to capture the promised 5x ROAS and rapidly grow top-line revenue before the competitor takes market share."

Where this memo agrees: the delivery app is worth entering, and waiting has a real cost. Risks of this plan names the chance that competitors take the launch window. The memo plans an app pilot of its own once the new baker is producing.

Where it differs: on the campaign itself and on what the growth rests on. The memo rejects the $15,000 upfront campaign. Your reason rests on the 5x, and the 5x is the agency's forecast for a channel you have never used. The agency is paid upfront whether it delivers or not, and the figure is revenue before the app's commission, ingredients and labor.

The strongest point against your call is that the campaign can't be both paid for and served. Production is maxed at $25,000, so new delivery orders need the baker. Campaign plus the baker's $8,000 through the two-month lead time comes to $23,000 against $20,000 in savings. And under the cap, the headroom is at most $17,000 a month, so the $75,000 can't arrive as fast growth. Orders you can't fill become cancellations and poor ratings on the very app you paid to launch on, which helps a competitor rather than holding them off.

What would show you are right: the agency puts the $75,000 on a timeframe that fits what you can bake, or ties its fee to results; you have funding beyond savings that covers the $3,000 gap plus a cushion; and there is evidence that a competitor is about to lock up the app in a way you couldn't win back by joining directly later. Without all of that, the cash math alone stands against the campaign.

The trade-off that is yours to weigh: how much of your savings you will risk on speed to market against a competitor. The memo doesn't include a competitor threat, because the question gives no facts about one. If you know of one, you are the only one who can judge how urgent it is.

Your second call: keep yours, switch, or change it, and the reason in a line.
