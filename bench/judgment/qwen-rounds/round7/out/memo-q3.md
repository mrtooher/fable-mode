## Recommendation

Reject the $15,000 regional campaign, and don't scale local ads either while production is maxed out at $25,000. Neither option fixes the real limit, which is capacity. Raise prices on the items that sell out first. Add the baker only if you still sell out at the new prices. After that, buy new demand with a capped delivery-app pilot of $3,000–$5,000, not $15,000 upfront. One condition changes the answer: if your sales records show you are clearly selling below $25,000 now, scaling local ads step by step is the right move. The $15,000 campaign stays rejected either way.

## Why

- **The campaign fails on cash before it fails on anything else.** $15,000 is 75% of your $20,000 savings and leaves $5,000. To serve any of the new demand you would also need the baker. Assuming you pay the $4,000 a month through the two-month lead time, that is $8,000 before any extra output. Campaign plus baker comes to $23,000, which is $3,000 more than you have.
- **The promised revenue doesn't fit the bakery.** At the stated premise that production is maxed out, none of the $75,000 can be baked until capacity is added. Even on the most generous reading, total revenue can't be above $25,000 and $8,000 of it is already ad-driven, so the headroom is at most $17,000 a month. $75,000 is more than four months of that. The agency gives no timeframe for it. Orders a new delivery listing can't fill turn into cancellations and poor ratings on the channel you were paying to build.
- **The 5x is a forecast and the 4x is a track record.** The 5x is the agency's promise for a channel you have never used, and the agency is paid upfront whether it delivers or not. Both figures are revenue, not profit. Delivery apps take a commission on each order, often around 15–30% (unverified — check the app's merchant terms). Ingredients and labor come out of what remains, so a 5x return on revenue can be close to break-even on margin.
- **More local ads hit the same ceiling.** At capacity, an extra Facebook dollar buys customers you have to turn away. Ad returns also typically fall as spend rises. The current 4x may already overstate what the ads add: if you would sell out anyway, some of the $8,000 credited to the ads is sales you would have made without them.
- **At the cap, the cheapest lever is price.** $25,000 is the units you can bake times your current prices. A price rise of 8–10% on the items that sell out raises the ceiling to $27,000–$27,500 at no cost in capacity, less whatever customers the higher prices lose. Whether a $4,000-a-month baker pays off depends on how much they add and on your margin. The added monthly sales times your contribution margin has to cover $4,000. For example, at a 40% margin that is $10,000 a month of added sales.

## What to do

1. **This week, confirm you are actually at the cap.** Pull the last three months of sales: total monthly revenue, and how often your top items sell out before close. Turn the agency down in writing. If they want the business, the only terms to consider are a pilot of $3,000–$5,000 or a fee tied to results, launched once the new capacity exists. Ask the delivery app directly about its self-serve launch promotions.
2. **If you are at or near $25,000 and selling out most days:** raise prices 8–10% on the items that sell out, and keep Facebook at $2,000 a month. Don't raise it.
3. **Decide by the end of week 4.** If you still sell out at the new prices, start the baker hire. That is about $8,000 through the lead time, which brings savings down to about $12,000. Before you commit, check the baker break-even from Why against your real margin. If you have stopped selling out, capacity is no longer binding at these prices. Don't hire. Roll prices back on any items that stopped selling out, and go to step 5.
4. **When the new baker is producing (about two months after the hire starts):** run the delivery-app pilot at $3,000–$5,000, sized to the added capacity. Sign with the app directly where you can. That leaves savings at about $7,000–$9,000, with the baker's $4,000 a month continuing. Expand only if pilot orders, after commission, cover ingredients, labor and the baker.
5. **If step 1 shows clear headroom (revenue well below $25,000, little selling out):** skip the price rise and scale local ads in steps of about $500 a month. Stop adding when a step's attributable revenue falls well below 4x, or when you get near $25,000. At that point, go back to step 2.

## Risks of this plan

- **The price rise could lose more customers than expected.** Revenue drops instead of rising. You can undo it within a week.
- **Waiting four weeks to decide on the baker delays new capacity by a month.** That is a month of sales you can't make if you truly sell out every day. If your records already show daily sellouts, starting the hire in week 1 is defensible.
- **The baker may not pay for themselves.** If the added output times your margin falls short of $4,000 a month, the hire is a steady drain, and the $8,000 spent during the lead time is gone either way.
- **Declining the agency may cost you a launch window on the app** if competitors get there first. The app is likely still open to you directly later, but the agency's specific package may not be.
- **The pilot may lose money if the app's commission is near the top of the 15–30% range.** It is capped at $3,000–$5,000 so it can't drain savings.
- **Cost of being wrong:** most of this plan is cheap to undo. Prices can be rolled back within a week, and Facebook stays where it is. The hard-to-undo part is the baker: about $8,000 is spent before any added output, and savings drop to about $12,000. If the counter-position below is right, you lose up to four weeks of growth on the price test. To recover, roll prices back and move to step 5.

## Strongest counter-position

*Capacity is not the binding constraint.* "Strictly maxed out at $25,000" may describe what the bakery *can* bake, not what it *sells*. The only sales figure given is the $8,000 attributed to ads. If total revenue is around $18,000, there is $7,000 a month of headroom. In that case, the incremental local-ads path the question offers is correct right now, with a proven 4x channel and no new hire. A price rise in a bakery that isn't selling out loses customers for nothing, and a baker would add cost to capacity you already don't use. This position wins if your sales records show monthly revenue clearly below $25,000 and top items still on the shelf at close. Those same records from step 1 show it either way.
