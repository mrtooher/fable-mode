## Core judgment
The panel will probably reject the $15,000 campaign and scale local ads step by step. Rejecting the campaign is right. Scaling local ads is wrong for the same reason. The question says production is "strictly maxed out at $25,000." If the bakery already sells that much, an extra ad dollar brings in demand it cannot bake for. The agency's $75,000 fails that test, and so does every new dollar from more Facebook ads. Spending more on ads at full capacity just buys turned-away customers and sold-out shelves. It also makes the 4x ROAS look better than it is, because sales the bakery could not make anyway get credited to the ads.

The campaign also fails on cash, even if you assume the extra output can be made. $15,000 upfront plus two months of baker pay ($4,000 × 2 = $8,000) comes to $23,000, more than the $20,000 in savings. And until the new baker is ready, the campaign creates demand the bakery has no way to fill. The 5x figure is a promise from the agency on a channel the bakery has never used. It is not evidence. Delivery apps also usually take commission on each order, often around 15–30% (unverified), so the ROAS overstates what the bakery keeps.

Neither offered option is the best one. **Treat the cap as a limit on output, not on revenue, and charge more for that output.** "$25,000" is units baked times current prices. A price rise of about 8–10% on the top sellers could lift the ceiling toward $27,000–$27,500 at no cost to capacity, minus any customers lost to the higher prices. At the same time, hold Facebook spend flat or cut it. Hire the baker only if demand at the new prices still sells out. A delivery-app channel only makes sense after that, as a small pilot paid month to month, not $15,000 upfront.

## Top risks
1. Scaling local ads at full capacity spends money on demand that cannot be served, and turned-away customers hurt word of mouth.
2. Approving the campaign uses 75% of savings ($15,000 of $20,000) on demand that cannot be filled for at least two months.
3. Campaign plus a hired baker needs about $23,000 against $20,000 in savings, which risks running out of cash.
4. Comparing revenue ROAS (4x vs 5x) hides delivery-app commissions and ingredient costs, so neither figure measures profit.
5. The current 4x may already overstate what the ads add if the bakery would sell out without them.

## Recommended change
This week, raise prices about 8–10% on the best sellers and cut Facebook spend by about $500 a month for four weeks. This costs nothing, and the cut saves $500 a month. I would change my mind if the bakery stops selling out (revenue falls clearly below the cap at the new prices). That would show capacity was never the real limit, and scaling local ads would make sense again.

## Confidence
Medium. I would want to confirm one thing first: the bakery is actually selling at or near $25,000 a month now, not just capable of it.

## Weakest assumption
That the bakery currently sells at the $25,000 cap. If it is really at, say, $18,000, then scaling local ads to fill the gap is reasonable, and my argument against it falls apart.
