## Your call and this memo

Your call: "Take the bulk discount to lock in the $9,000 savings and improve gross margins before the competitor arrives."

**Where this memo agrees:** the discount is worth having, and getting a lower unit cost in place before the competitor opens is a sound aim. The memo does not recommend giving up the 15%. Full-price monthly ordering is only its fallback.

**Where it differs:** you would take the offer as it stands, a $51,000 six-month order paid upfront. The memo would not. It would ask for the same discount with delivery and payment monthly.

**The strongest point against your call:** the order costs $51,000 and you hold $40,000. That leaves an $11,000 gap, and you would start month 1 with no reserve four months before a confirmed drop in traffic. You would also not be locking in $9,000. Storage takes $3,000, so the most the deal can save is $6,000, before interest on the $11,000. If sales in months 5–6 fall in line with the typical 20% traffic drop, about $4,000 of stock is left over, and the saving falls to $2,000–$6,000 depending on whether that surplus sells. The margin gain also doesn't need the prepayment. A four-month or six-month commitment paid monthly gets the same 15% per unit without the cash gap or the storage. A gross-margin figure can show the full 15% while the storage and interest are booked below it, so the margin looks better than the cash outcome.

**What would show you are right:** the supplier's written terms allowing 60 or more interest-free days to pay; recent sales receipts showing the $11,000 is covered with at least two months of fixed costs (rent, payroll) still in hand; goods that keep, so any surplus carries into month 7; and the supplier turning down every monthly-payment version. If all of those hold, the prepaid order saves about $6,000, and taking it is the better call.

**The trade-off that is yours to weigh:** how much cash cushion you want going into the competitor's opening, against a saving of $2,000–$6,000 (not $9,000) from prepaying. That depends on your risk tolerance and on cash figures the question doesn't give, so it is your call to make.

Your second call: keep yours, switch, or change it, and the reason in a line.
