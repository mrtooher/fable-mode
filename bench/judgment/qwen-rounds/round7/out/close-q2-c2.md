## Your call and this memo

Your call: "Invest in the AI software to immediately eliminate $3,200 of monthly labor costs and achieve an 80% reduction in processing time."

**Where the memo agrees:** automate. Over the two-year term, automation beats scaling manually. Manual scaling costs about $164,700, against $16,800 for the software. The software breaks even at about 10% real savings (growth case) or 17.5% (flat volume). On direction, you and the memo are on the same side.

**Where the memo differs:** on "immediately" and on the $3,200.
- **Immediately.** The memo says don't pay the $12,000 lump sum from a $15,000 account or sign the two-year contract as written. Paying now leaves $3,000, which is less than one month of the bookkeeper alone. Exiting after three months would cost $18,600, more than the whole account.
- **The $3,200.** That figure is 80% of $4,000, and it holds only if three things are true. The full 80% shows up on day one. The bookkeeper is cut to about 4 hours a week. And the $200 monthly fee is ignored, which brings the net to $3,000. The memo instead keeps her at 20 hours, because she sets the tool up and checks its output. On that path, year one roughly breaks even and the gain comes in year two.

**The strongest point against your call:** the plan assumes the savings arrive at once, in full, and as cash. But small firms typically see 20–50% after a 2–3 month ramp (a figure to check with the vendor's reference customers). Cutting her hours would also likely lose the person the tool depends on. So the first months could bring the full $12,000 outlay with little offsetting saving, at exactly the point the account has only $3,000 left. One late client payment would then put payroll at risk.

**What would show you're right:**
- Receivables due within 30 days plus committed credit keep the account above two months of total operating costs after the $12,000 payment.
- The vendor's reference firms of your size report savings near 80%, without a long ramp.

If both hold, the memo's own counter-position applies: sign now, because every month of delay gives up as much as $3,000.

**The trade-off only you can weigh:** how much liquidity risk the firm can stomach to start saving sooner. Each month of negotiating or piloting gives up savings. The memo's conditions protect payroll. You know the firm's real cash position and the bookkeeper better than this memo does.

Your second call: keep yours, switch, or change it, and the reason in a line.
