## Core judgment
Reject the $15,000 campaign as proposed. A third option beats both offered ones: a capped pilot. Sign up directly with the delivery app, with no agency or a performance-based fee. Cap the spend at about $3-5k. Time the launch to when capacity exists.

Capacity makes the 5x claim moot. The cap is $25,000 total monthly revenue. Current revenue is at least $8,000 from ads, and likely more, because the question doesn't give total revenue. So the most new revenue the bakery can fill is under $17,000 a month, and probably far less. The promised $75,000 is therefore more than 4x the bakery's maximum monthly headroom, and the question gives no timeframe for it. Any revenue above the cap can't be baked. Orders turned away on a new app also hurt the listing's ratings, which is the bakery's main growth lever on a platform.

The cash exposure is also lopsided. $15,000 upfront is 75% of the $20,000 savings. The baker costs $4,000 a month, with two months of lead time before capacity rises. If the campaign works, the bakery would have to fund the hire from the remaining $5,000. If it underdelivers, it has lost most of its reserves. The ROAS is revenue, not profit. After ingredients, labor and the app's commission (typically 15-30%, unverified here), the margin on delivery orders is thin. A 5x ROAS on revenue could be near break-even once the $15,000 and the added baker are counted.

Scaling local ads incrementally is safer but hits the same ceiling. Ad ROAS usually falls as spend rises, and it is useless once production is full. Capacity is the binding constraint. Fix it first, or at least in parallel.

## Top risks
- Capacity overflow: demand above $25k a month gets turned away, which hurts ratings and wastes paid acquisition.
- Liquidity: $15k out the door plus $4k a month of payroll could leave the bakery unable to cover a slow month on $5k.
- Agency forecasts of this kind are typically optimistic and rarely guaranteed. A 5x promise with no refund or make-good clause is a marketing claim, not a contract.
- Margin erosion: app commissions, packaging and delivery-order discounts could turn "5x ROAS" into a loss on contribution margin.
- Cannibalization and dependence: app orders often shift existing customers to a higher-fee channel instead of adding new ones.

## Recommended change
Do not pay the agency. Within one week, ask the delivery app directly for its self-serve launch promotions and onboarding credits. If you do use the agency, offer a smaller scope: a pilot of $3-5k, or fees tied to results, and a launch that starts about two months after you start the baker hire, so capacity matches demand. I would change my mind if the agency accepts a performance-based fee or a refund clause and bookings show real headroom under the $25k cap, or if current monthly revenue is low enough that headroom is large.

## Confidence
Medium. I'd want your actual current monthly total revenue and contribution margin per delivery order checked first, since the headroom and break-even math depend on them.

## Weakest assumption
That current total monthly revenue is close enough to the $25,000 cap that little headroom exists, because the question gives only the $8,000 ad-attributed figure.
