## Core judgment
Do not take the six-month deal as offered. It costs $51,000 and you hold $40,000, so it needs about $11,000 of borrowing or a payables stretch. The $9,000 headline also overstates the saving. Off-site storage is up to $3,000 ($500 x 6), so the net is about $6,000 (the storage figure is a ceiling because stock depletes). The real trade is a roughly 12% return on cash locked up for up to six months. In exchange you give up liquidity right before a competitor arrives. That is a poor trade for a store with one month of runway beyond the commitment.

The competitor opens in month 4 and typically cuts foot traffic about 20%, so months 5 and 6 need roughly $8,000 of stock, not $10,000. Prepaid inventory ties up about $4,000 of excess (my estimate, assuming inventory need falls in proportion to traffic), and the store has no cash to run promotions, adjust pricing or cover a margin squeeze. Small stores facing a new nearby competitor typically need cash for markdowns, loyalty offers and slower turns. Cash is the scarce asset, and the discount spends it.

A better option is missing from the framing: negotiate a shorter commitment. Ask for the 15% (or a smaller discount, such as 10%) on a 3-month order covering months 1-3, before the competitor opens. That costs about $25,500 ($30,000 x 0.85), saves about $4,500 gross, and leaves roughly $14,500 in cash. Better still, ask the supplier to hold the goods in its own warehouse and ship monthly against your prepayment, which removes the storage fee. Otherwise stay on monthly ordering.

## Top risks
- Cash shortfall: $51,000 exceeds your $40,000, so you must borrow or stretch payables just as sales are about to fall.
- Overstock: the 20% traffic drop in months 5-6 leaves about $4,000 of unneeded stock plus carrying cost (my estimate).
- No cash buffer for competitive response (promotions, price matching, staffing), and no allowance for rent, payroll or other fixed costs in your figures.
- Storage costs, shrinkage or spoilage, and dead stock if demand shifts to different items.
- The traffic drop may exceed 20% or last longer than "typical", which makes the overstock worse.

## Recommended change
Call the supplier this week and counter with a 3-month prepaid order at the same or a slightly lower discount, delivered in monthly tranches from their warehouse. I would change my mind only if you can finance the full $51,000 cheaply and still hold a cash buffer of at least two months of fixed costs after the payment.

## Confidence
High — the arithmetic on affordability and net saving checks against your figures, and the direction (don't commit the full six months) holds even if the 20% traffic estimate is off.

## Weakest assumption
That the supplier will accept a shorter, smaller prepaid order at a meaningful discount; if it won't, the fallback is flexible monthly ordering, not the full bulk deal.
