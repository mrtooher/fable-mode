## Your call and this memo

Your call: "Maintain the current staffing model to preserve the $15,000 operating cash and retain the flexibility to scale labor with the 15% quarterly growth."

**Where this memo agrees.** On cash, fully. It too says don't pay $12,000 from a $15,000 account, because that leaves $3,000, less than a month of the bookkeeper alone. It also keeps the bookkeeper. And in the short term your call matches the memo's own fallback: if the vendor refuses every concession and no credit comes through, stay manual for up to two quarters while cash builds.

**Where it differs.** The memo treats your cash reason as a reason not to pay the lump sum, not a reason to stay manual for good. Spread payments, a line of credit or a month-to-month tool protect the $15,000 just as well. On flexibility, the memo's figures point the other way. Hours reach about 40 a week around month 15. After that, scaling labor means hiring a second bookkeeper, which the memo calls hard to unwind. By month 24 the work is about 61 hours a week.

**The strongest point against your call.** Scaling manually costs about $164,700 over 24 months. The software costs $16,800 over the term and breaks even at about 10% real savings under growth, or 17.5% if volume stays flat. Even at 30% savings, well below the vendor's 80%, the memo puts the two-year saving at about $32,600. Preserving cash today is likely to cost more cash later.

**What would show you're right:**
- a pilot or reference firms showing real savings below about 10–17.5%;
- growth stalling well short of 15% a quarter;
- no installments, no pilot exit, no credit and no month-to-month alternative, so that automating would mean the lump sum;
- bookkeeping billed hourly and clients who won't move to fixed fees, so that the savings pass to them.

**Yours to weigh.** How much cash cushion the firm needs now, set against a likely larger two-year saving, depends on how much risk you can carry. That is your judgment, not the memo's.

Your second call: keep yours, switch, or change it, and the reason in a line.
