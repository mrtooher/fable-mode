## Your call and this memo

Your call: "Reject the regional campaign and incrementally scale local ads, ensuring marketing spend never exceeds current, fulfillable production capacity."

**Where the memo agrees:** reject the $15,000 campaign. On that part you and the memo reach the same answer for the same reason: the bakery can't bake the demand the campaign is meant to buy. The memo also agrees with your guardrail. Marketing spend should never buy more demand than you can fill. If your sales records show clear headroom below $25,000, your plan is the memo's step 5: add about $500 a month at a time, and stop when a step falls well below 4x or you get near $25,000.

**Where it differs:** the memo's main case assumes you are already at or near the $25,000 cap. Under that assumption, your own guardrail means "scale local ads" stops at step zero. Every extra Facebook dollar buys customers you have to turn away, so the plan has nothing to scale. The strongest point against your call is that it gives you no way past $25,000. It protects the ceiling but never raises it. At the cap, the memo's levers are price (8–10% on the items that sell out, toward $27,000–$27,500 with no added capacity) and then the baker. More ad spend isn't one of them.

**What would show you are right:** your last three months of sales from step 1 show monthly revenue clearly below $25,000, with top items still on the shelf at close. In that case scaling local ads step by step is the right move now, and the price rise and the baker can wait.

**What only you can weigh:** whether to grow past $25,000 at all. That growth costs about $8,000 of your $20,000 savings for the baker before any added output, and the baker may not pay for themselves. Staying at capacity and protecting cash is a fair choice. If you make it, the memo's step 2 (keep Facebook at $2,000 and raise prices on what sells out) still holds.
