## Core judgment
Automation is probably the right direction, but you shouldn't sign this contract as written. The headline math is strong. The bookkeeper costs 20 h x $50 x 4 weeks = $4,000 a month. An 80% cut saves $3,200, or about $3,000 after the $200 fee, so the $12,000 pays back in about 4 months. The two-year cost is $12,000 + 24 x $200 = $16,800. That breaks even at only about $700 a month of savings, or roughly 17.5% of current labor, even with flat volume. The 15% quarterly growth strengthens the case. Volume roughly doubles in 5 quarters (1.15^5 = 2.01) and is about 3x in 8 quarters. Scaling manually would mean going from 20 hours to about 60 hours a week of bookkeeping by the end of the contract.

The real trade-off is cash and lock-in, not ROI. Paying $12,000 from a $15,000 account leaves $3,000, which is less than one month of the bookkeeper's pay. You would keep paying the bookkeeper through implementation and parallel running, so there is no cash to absorb a slow ramp. The $6,000 exit fee is more than the $3,000 you'd have left, so you couldn't afford to leave either. Typically, small firms see real savings of 20-50% rather than the vendor's 80%, and it takes 2-3 months to get there (typical, not universal). At the 17.5% breakeven you would still come out ahead, so the cost of the tool isn't the danger. The danger is a liquidity crisis during the ramp.

The option missing from the framing is a negotiated or staged adoption. Ask the vendor for a 60-90 day paid pilot or an out clause, with the $12,000 split into installments or financed through a line of credit. Run the tool in parallel on real client files, then cut the bookkeeper's hours gradually. A cheaper month-to-month tool is the fallback if the vendor won't move.

## Top risks
- Cash squeeze: $3,000 left after payment, with payroll continuing during the ramp.
- The 80% claim fails on your actual client mix, so savings are only 20-40% and ramp slowly.
- Lock-in: the $6,000 exit fee plus 24 months of fees if the tool underperforms.
- Bookkeeper departure or an accuracy and review gap, since an accounting firm can't ship unchecked books.
- Growth stalls, which shrinks the volume advantage (a lower risk, because breakeven is only about 17.5% savings at current volume).

## Recommended change
Before signing, ask for a 60-90 day pilot or exit clause, installment or financed upfront payment, and a waiver or reduction of the cancellation fee. Run the tool on 3-5 real client files for 2-4 weeks and measure actual hours saved. I would change my mind and sign roughly as-is only if the vendor refuses all concessions and you can secure at least $10-15k of extra cash or credit and the pilot shows 40% or more savings; if the pilot shows under 20%, pass.

## Confidence
Medium — I'd want the vendor's pilot or trial results on your own client files before staking much on it.

## Weakest assumption
That the software actually delivers close to the promised 80% time reduction on this firm's real workflow, without a long ramp or heavy review overhead.
